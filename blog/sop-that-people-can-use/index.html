Write an SOP people can use during a busy shift

Turn a long policy document into a usable sequence with ownership, exceptions and version control.

## Write for the task An SOP should help a trained person perform a specific process consistently. State when it starts, what inputs are required and what counts as completion. Keep policy rationale separate from the steps needed at the workstation. ## Make each step observable Use an action, responsible role and confirmation point. "Receive carefully" is vague. "Compare the handling-unit count with the manifest and record discrepancies before acceptance" describes something that can be checked. ## Include the exception path Explain what to do when a label is unreadable, a system is unavailable or the quantity differs. Name the escalation role and identify where work should wait. Without this path, staff invent incompatible workarounds under pressure. ## Test and maintain Ask a trained user who did not write the document to walk through it. Capture unclear instructions, update the version and remove obsolete copies. Review after meaningful process changes, not only at a calendar anniversary. ## Further reading [Lean Enterprise Institute — Standardized Work](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/standardized-work/) *Practical editorial guide.
