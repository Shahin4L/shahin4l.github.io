Protect dispatch cutoffs with readiness checkpoints

Work backwards from departure and make the last responsible decision visible before the vehicle leaves.

## Departure is the final event An on-time truck depends on several earlier completions: final sort, manifest validation, loading and handover. Publish a readiness sequence rather than announcing only the departure deadline. ## Work backwards Define the latest acceptable time for each milestone using observed duration and a realistic buffer. Name the person who confirms each step. Record late incoming work separately so that it does not quietly delay every committed shipment. ## An illustrative schedule For a 04:00 departure, a team might reserve 20 minutes for the final handover and 30 minutes for loading, placing loading start at 03:10. Those durations are examples, not universal targets; validate them against site conditions. ## Measure what was promised On-time departure rate should use all eligible planned departures in the denominator. Explain cancellation and rescheduling rules in advance. After a miss, compare planned and actual checkpoints to identify where recovery time disappeared, then change one controllable cause. ## Further reading [MIT CTL — FreightLab](https://ctl.mit.edu/research/freightlab) *Practical editorial guide.
