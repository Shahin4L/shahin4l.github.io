A practical linehaul control tower for small teams

Use milestone-based visibility to coordinate departures, arrival risks and recovery actions.

## Visibility should trigger coordination A control tower can begin with one dependable trip register. It needs planned milestones, actual events, a current risk and an owner. A moving map is useful only when the underlying event data is trustworthy. ## Keep the trip record small Record route, vehicle reference, planned departure, actual departure, last verified update and destination receipt. Mark estimated arrival as an estimate and show when it was last revised. Avoid publishing driver contact details in a public dashboard. ## Act on the next constraint When a late arrival threatens the receiving shift, confirm the estimate, notify the receiving lead and check unloading capacity. Escalate the operational consequence, not just the late timestamp. Avoid promising a recovery time that has not been validated. ## Learn route by route Compare transit time and dwell time separately. Review the spread of journey times as well as the average. A reliable short route and a volatile long route need different planning buffers; a single network-wide target can conceal that difference. ## Further reading [MIT CTL — FreightLab](https://ctl.mit.edu/research/freightlab) *Practical editorial guide.
