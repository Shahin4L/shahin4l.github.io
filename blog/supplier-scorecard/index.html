Build a supplier scorecard both sides can understand

Evaluate delivery, quality and responsiveness with agreed definitions rather than a mysterious composite score.

## Start from the agreement Measure what the supplier actually committed to deliver. Define order completeness, the promised delivery window, acceptance criteria and issue-response expectations. A scorecard should make improvement discussions clearer for both parties. ## Keep components visible Use a small number of measures: delivery against confirmed dates, accepted quantity, recurring defects and response to open issues. Show the eligible order count and reporting period. Ten perfect small orders should not conceal one critical incomplete order. ## Handle changes fairly If your team changes a requirement or confirms a new delivery date, retain the original record and the agreed revision. Separate supplier-caused delays from internal approval delays. Record disputed observations rather than silently overwriting them. ## Turn review into improvement A weighted total can help summarise performance, but publish its weights and retain the component results. Pick one recurring gap, agree a corrective action and review it at the next meeting. Treat a new supplier's limited history as uncertainty, not proof of reliability. ## Further reading [World Bank — Procurement Framework](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/what-we-do/project-procurement/framework) *Practical editorial guide.
