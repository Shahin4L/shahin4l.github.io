A short shift handover that protects continuity

Give the next shift the operating picture, unresolved risks and ownership needed to continue safely and consistently.

## Handover is more than a summary The next lead needs to know what changed and what remains unresolved. A long list of yesterday's achievements can hide the one issue that will block the next hour. Keep the exchange focused on continuity. ## Use a repeatable agenda - Current volume and work remaining by stage. - Equipment or system constraints affecting capacity. - Priority commitments and unresolved exceptions. - Staff coverage and who owns the next decision. ## Confirm understanding For a critical open item, ask the receiving lead to repeat the next action and update time. Link to the controlled record rather than copying customer information into a broad chat group. Record ownership transfer explicitly. ## Improve the meeting Review whether the next shift discovered an important issue that was missing from handover. Add that information to the template. Remove repetitive fields that no one uses, while keeping required checks intact. A short meeting succeeds when it transfers useful context, not when it merely ends quickly. ## Further reading [Lean Enterprise Institute — Standardized Work](https://www.lean.org/lexicon-terms/standardized-work/) *Practical editorial guide.
