Plan shift capacity around the real bottleneck

Translate incoming volume into workable station hours, then test whether the whole process can keep up.

## Look beyond headcount Ten available people do not create equal capacity at every station. Scanning, sorting and loading have different work content, equipment limits and dependencies. Observe a representative period before setting a planning rate. ## Convert demand into hours Required productive hours = planned units ÷ observed sustainable units per productive hour. State what is excluded from productive time: breaks, setup and unavoidable meetings still need coverage in the roster. ## Test the constraint For an illustrative 4,800 units at 160 units per hour, the work requires 30 productive hours. Six people with five productive hours each match that arithmetic. They do not guarantee completion if a downstream station can accept only 600 units per hour over the same five hours. ## Run a small trial Compare planned arrivals with actual arrivals by time slot. Move cross-trained staff only when the receiving station is ready.
