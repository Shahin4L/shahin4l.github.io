Root cause analysis: move from blame to evidence

Use a precise problem statement and testable causes to make corrective actions more useful.

## Describe the failure precisely "The team was careless" is not an investigation. Start with the expected condition, actual condition, location, time window and affected quantity. Preserve the evidence needed to distinguish competing explanations. ## Generate, then test A fishbone diagram can organise possible causes across methods, people, equipment, material and information. Its branches are hypotheses, not conclusions. Check records, observe the process and ask what evidence would disprove the preferred explanation. ## Keep containment separate Temporarily double-checking a label may reduce immediate risk. It does not explain why the wrong label was produced. Record the immediate containment, verified cause and lasting corrective action as three separate items. ## Verify the change Assign an owner and a review window. Compare defect recurrence under similar conditions and inspect whether the new method is actually being used. If the issue returns, reopen the explanation rather than declaring that staff simply need another reminder. ## Further reading [ASQ — Fishbone Diagrams](https://asq.org/quality-resources/fishbone) *Practical editorial guide.
