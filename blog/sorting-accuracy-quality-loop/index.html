Improve sorting accuracy without slowing the flow

Combine clear routing rules, readable labels and feedback at the point where errors originate.

## Define the defect first A mis-sort might mean a wrong destination, a wrong container or an item sent without its required scan. Give each defect a separate reason code. Count affected unique items, not every repeated error notification. ## Build prevention into the station Keep destination names consistent across the screen, shelf and manifest. Provide a visible exception lane for unreadable labels or uncertain routing. For barcode systems, check identification rules against the relevant GS1 standard rather than inventing incompatible identifiers. ## Use two measures Track confirmed mis-sorts per eligible sorted items, and separately track the time taken to resolve exceptions. For example, 12 affected items among 6,000 sorted items is a 0.2% observed defect rate. That rate depends on detection coverage and is not proof that every other item was correct. ## Close the loop Review the leading defect reason with the station team. Try one improvement, such as clearer destination separation, then compare equivalent shifts. Keep volume and quality visible together. ## Further reading [GS1 — Barcode Standards](https://www2.gs1.org/standards/barcodes) *Practical editorial guide.
