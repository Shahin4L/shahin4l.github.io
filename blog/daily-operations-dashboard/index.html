A daily operations dashboard that leads to action

Choose a few well-defined measures, expose exceptions and attach every problem to a next step.

## Start with a decision A dashboard earns its place when a supervisor can use it to decide what to do next. Begin with three questions: what arrived, what was completed and what remains at risk? Keep the reporting window and time zone visible. ## Build an action view - Show opening backlog, new arrivals, completed work and closing backlog. - Separate volume, service, quality and cost measures. - Put the exception, responsible owner and next update time beside each red indicator. - Define whether each quantity counts unique parcels or scan events. ## A worked example If opening backlog is 120, arrivals are 800 and valid completions are 760, closing backlog should reconcile to 160. Transfers and cancellations need separate, signed adjustments. Repeated scans must not inflate completion volume. ## Start tomorrow Choose one shift, reconcile the totals against source records and ask the lead to name one action prompted by the report. Remove indicators that never change a decision. Add trend comparisons only after the daily definitions are stable. ## Further reading [ASQ — Plan-Do-Check-Act](https://asq.org/quality-resources/pdca-cycle) *Practical editorial guide.
