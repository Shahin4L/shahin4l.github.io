Start demand forecasting with a baseline you can challenge

Separate ordinary demand patterns from events and compare forecasting methods on unseen periods.

## Begin with a simple benchmark Before using a complex model, compare demand with the same weekday in recent weeks. Keep promotions, holidays and disruptions in an event calendar. A forecast describes uncertainty; it is not a guaranteed arrival plan. ## Separate development and evaluation Use earlier periods to build the method and later periods to test it. Never let the future leak into the calculation. Record the forecast as it existed before actual volume became known, rather than replacing it afterward. ## Measure error consistently Mean absolute error uses the average absolute difference between forecast and actual volume. Also track signed error to see persistent over- or under-forecasting. Percentage errors can become misleading when actual demand is zero or very small. ## Connect the forecast to a decision Agree what happens if demand is above or below the expected range: staffing options, equipment readiness or a recovery trigger. Compare model complexity with operational benefit.
