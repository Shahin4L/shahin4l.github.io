Cost per parcel: define the denominator before comparing teams

Make scope, allocation and volume rules visible so unit-cost comparisons support fair decisions.

## A small ratio hides big choices Cost per parcel = included cost ÷ eligible parcel volume. The arithmetic is easy; the scope is the difficult part. Decide whether you are measuring sorting cost, delivery cost or end-to-end cost before comparing results. ## Match cost and activity Use the same period and operating scope for the numerator and denominator. Explain how shared rent, transport and support salaries are allocated. Distinguish unique parcels from repeated handling events so rework does not make productivity look better. ## An illustrative comparison If a department's included monthly cost is BDT 120,000 and it handles 60,000 eligible unique parcels, its measured cost is BDT 2 per parcel. That is not automatically the company's total delivery cost. ## Interpret changes carefully A lower unit cost can follow higher volume even when absolute spending rises. Compare service, damage and backlog alongside cost. Report allocation changes openly and avoid ranking teams whose work mix or service obligations differ materially. ## Further reading [ASQ — Plan-Do-Check-Act](https://asq.org/quality-resources/pdca-cycle) *Practical editorial guide.
