Procurement value goes beyond the quoted price

Compare fit, lifecycle cost, delivery reliability and risk before choosing the lowest quotation.

## Define the need before comparing offers A cheaper product can become expensive if it fails the actual task. Write the required function, expected workload, acceptance criteria and support needs before asking for quotations. Keep optional features separate from mandatory requirements. ## Build a comparable evaluation Use the same quantity, delivery location and scope for every supplier. Compare purchase price with consumables, maintenance, implementation and expected usable life. Document assumptions instead of presenting uncertain future savings as facts. ## A small example Two printers may have different upfront prices and cartridge yields. Calculate cost per expected page using consistent assumptions, then test print quality and compatibility. A lower calculated cost does not compensate for failing a mandatory requirement. ## Make the choice reviewable Agree evaluation criteria before opening offers, retain the decision record and identify conflicts of interest. World Bank guidance offers a useful background on value for money; its project rules do not automatically apply to every private purchase. ## Further reading [World Bank — Procurement Framework](https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/what-we-do/project-procurement/framework) *Practical editorial guide.
